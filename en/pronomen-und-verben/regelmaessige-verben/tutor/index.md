## Context: the page “Regular Verbs in the Present Tense”

Conjugate regular German verbs such as wohnen, lernen, spielen, arbeiten, and heißen in the present tense.

### First School Day in Year 6

On Monday Peter starts Year 6 in Lindenberg with Lukas. They meet Nadia: *Ich wohne hier, ich lerne Deutsch und wir spielen zusammen Tischtennis.*

### The Endings: -e, -st, -t, -en

Picture: Verb builder for machen: ich mache, du machst, er/sie/es macht, wir machen, ihr macht, sie/Sie machen, with endings -e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en highlighted. Caption: The stem (mach-) stays the same — only the ending changes for each person.

Regular conjugation with wohnen (to live):
- Pronoun | Ending | wohnen
- ich | -e | wohn**e**
- du | -st | wohn**st**
- er / sie / es | -t | wohn**t**
- wir | -en | wohn**en**
- ihr | -t | wohn**t**
- sie / Sie | -en | wohn**en**

**Remember: Two Small Spelling Helpers**

If the stem ends in **-t** or **-d** (*arbeit-en*, *find-en*), add an extra **-e-** before *-st* and *-t*: *du arbeit**e**st*, *er arbeit**e**t*, *ihr arbeit**e**t*. If the stem ends in **-s, -ß, -z** (*heiß-en*), *du* adds only **-t**: *du heiß**t***.

Vocabulary (German – English): Common regular verbs in Lindenberg
- wohnen – to live (reside). Example: Peter wohnt jetzt in Lindenberg. – Peter now lives in Lindenberg.
- lernen – to learn, to study. Example: Wir lernen Deutsch. – We are learning German.
- arbeiten – to work. Example: Frau Weber arbeitet im Büro. – Frau Weber works in an office.
- heißen – to be called. Example: Wie heißt du? – What is your name?

### Exercise regelmaessig-einstieg

- Task: The infinitive is *lernen* (stem: *lern-*). What is the form for *du*?
- Answer format: choose one answer
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ du lernst – Correct: stem lern- plus ending -st for du.
  - ✗ du lernt – -t is the ending for er/sie/es and ihr, not for du.
  - ✗ du lerne – -e is the ending for ich.
- Solution: du lernst
- Explanation: Remove -en from the infinitive to find the verb stem (lernen → lern-), then add the personal ending.

### Exercise regelmaessig-zuordnen

- Task: Match each pronoun to the right form of *spielen* or *arbeiten*.
- Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
- Solution: ich – spiele; du – spielst; er / sie / es – arbeitet; wir / sie / Sie – spielen
- Explanation: ich takes -e, du takes -st, er/sie/es takes -t (with extra -e- after -t: arbeitet), and wir and sie/Sie take -en.

### Exercise regelmaessig-luecken

- Task: Conjugate the verbs in parentheses in the present tense.
- Answer format: fill in the gaps in German
- How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters right with a hint, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation wrong
- Items (start → solution):
1. Nadia plays basketball. (spielen) → Nadia spielt Basketball.
     - spielen: Nadia is 3rd person singular (sie): stem spiel- + -t.
2. You work a lot, Herr Weber! (arbeiten) → Herr Weber, Sie arbeiten viel!
     - arbeitet: Formal Sie always takes -en: Sie arbeiten.
3. What is your name? (heißen, du) → Wie heißt du?
     - heißst: After -ß, the s of -st is dropped: du heißt.
4. He works in Lindenberg. (arbeiten) → Er arbeitet in Lindenberg.
     - arbeitt: After a stem ending in -t, insert an extra -e-: er arbeitet.
- Explanation: With the pattern -e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en (plus extra -e- after -t/-d and -t after -s/-ß/-z), you can conjugate almost all regular verbs.
- How to help: the app checks word by word against the solutions above and names mistakes of form (e.g. accents) with the child's words. Do not give the solution: ask about the rule (ending, gender, tense) or about a similar sentence of the page. If the app takes a version of the child's as wrong that you think is right, tell the child: the app knows only the versions above.

### Exercise regelmaessig-schreiben

- Task: Translate these short sentences into German.
- Answer format: write the whole answer in German
- How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters wrong, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation ignored
- Items (start → solution):
1. I live in Lindenberg. → Ich wohne in Lindenberg.

2. We learn German. → Wir lernen Deutsch.

- Explanation: German has no separate '-ing' tense — the present tense covers both 'I learn' and 'I am learning'.
