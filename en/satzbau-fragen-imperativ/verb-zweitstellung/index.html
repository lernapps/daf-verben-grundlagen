The Fixed Position-2 Verb Anchor

Peter plans his week in Lindenberg. German statements follow one iron rule: the conjugated verb stays locked in Position 2 — no matter which Sentence Card moves to the front.

Exercise

Which German sentence is correct when *Am Montag* (On Monday) sits in Position 1?

  • Am Montag lernt Peter Deutsch. right answer

    Correct: Am Montag is one Sentence Card (Position 1), lernt stays in Position 2, and Peter follows right after.

  • Am Montag Peter lernt Deutsch.

    That follows English word order — in German it pushes lernt into Position 3, which is wrong.

  • Am Montag Deutsch lernt Peter.

    Only one Sentence Card may stand before the verb, not two (Am Montag + Deutsch).

Position 1 holds one whole phrase (e.g. Heute or Am Montag). The conjugated verb always comes immediately next.

What Happens When Something Else Goes First?

Verb-second order (V2)

In every German statement, the conjugated verb stands in Position 2. If a time or place card moves to Position 1, the subject jumps right behind the verb.

Swap Position 1 — Position 2 stays locked
Position 1Position 2 (Verb)Rest of sentence
Minaspieltheute im Sportverein.
HeutespieltMina im Sportverein.
Im SportvereinspieltMina heute.

Practice

Exercise

Check the Sentence Cards: in which sentences is the conjugated verb properly in Position 2?

  • Am Dienstag spielt Lukas Fußball im Park. right answer

    Correct: Am Dienstag (Pos. 1) + spielt (Pos. 2) + Lukas (subject).

  • Nach der Schule Peter fährt mit dem Fahrrad.

    Incorrect: fährt is pushed to Position 3. Correct: Nach der Schule fährt Peter mit dem Fahrrad.

  • Im Park treffen wir Nadia und Mina. right answer

    Correct: Im Park (Pos. 1) + treffen (Pos. 2) + wir (subject).

Always count Sentence Cards: no matter how many words fill Position 1 (e.g. Nach der Schule), the verb comes immediately next.

Exercise

Rewrite each sentence starting with the given phrase in Position 1. Keep the verb in Position 2!

  1. Peter reads a book today. → Start with: Heute
  2. We eat rolls in Lindenberg. → Start with: In Lindenberg
Solution
  1. Heute liest Peter ein Buch.
  2. In Lindenberg essen wir Brötchen.

After any time or place phrase at the start of a German sentence, the Verb Anchor comes immediately, never the subject.
